Week by week

Eight weeks, one growing world.

Nothing is thrown away between projects. The creature from week one is the hero of the story in week four and the star of the trailer in week seven.

Project 01 · Weeks 1–2

Invent a creature

A character with its own powers, habitat and visual identity.

  1. Week 1From head to page

    Sketch the creature on paper, then describe it precisely enough that the AI draws what you pictured, not what it guessed.

  2. Week 2Powers, habitat, identity

    Set the rules of the creature’s world, compare versions side by side and choose the one that’s truly yours.

They practise
  • Describing precisely
  • Working within constraints
  • Iterating
Project 02 · Weeks 3–5

Write its adventure

A short illustrated story with the creature as its hero.

  1. Week 3The shape of a story

    A beginning, a problem, a turning point and an ending, mapped out before anything is generated.

  2. Week 4Draft and illustrate

    Write the scenes and illustrate them, keeping the creature recognisable from page to page.

  3. Week 5Edit like an author

    Read it aloud, cut what drags and fix what the AI got wrong.

They practise
  • Story structure
  • Cause and effect
  • Editing
Project 03 · Weeks 6–7

Bring the world to life

A short narrated trailer for the universe they’ve built.

  1. Week 6Storyboard the trailer

    Pick the moments that matter, put them in order and write the narration, helping each other along the way.

  2. Week 7Narrate and assemble

    Record the voice-over, sequence the scenes and rehearse the presentation.

They practise
  • Storyboarding
  • Sequencing
  • Teamwork
  • Presenting
Week 8

The showcase

Parents are invited. Each child presents their creature, story and trailer, and explains what was their own idea, what the AI added, what went wrong and what they changed.

What your child practises

Four habits that outlast any tool.

The tools will keep changing. What we teach is the thinking around them, and children get to practise it in every session.

Express

Turn the picture in their head into words precise enough for someone else, or something else, to act on.

Experiment

Try it, change one thing, try again. Constraints turn it into a game.

Evaluate

Look hard at a result and decide whether it’s really what they meant.

Improve

Edit, cut and rework until the piece is theirs, then stand up and present it.
