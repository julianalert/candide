Week by week

Five phases, one mission a week.

From comic to film, animate, voice and sound, edit, premiere. Every week ends with something concrete saved on the platform.

  1. 01
    How animation worksMake a 10-page paper flipbook of their hero doing one movement, and see that a film is still pictures shown fast. Then watch AI turn one comic panel into a moving clip.AI skill · AI video guesses the frames between pictures
    Paper flipbook
  2. 02
    From comic to scriptChoose which panels become shots and add what a comic can’t show: movement, sound, voices. Write a simple script. AI asks feedback questions and never rewrites.AI skill · adapting a story from one medium to another
    Film script
  3. 03
    Shot list and animaticLearn shot types and camera moves, fill in a shot list on paper, then place the comic panels on a timeline to preview the film as a slideshow.AI skill · planning before generating (little AI this week)
    Shot list + animatic
  4. 04
    Make your hero moveTest shots: animate the hero waving, running and jumping from their character sheet, and find out what AI video does badly: hands, fast spins, crowds.AI skill · motion prompts: who, action, camera, speed
    3 test clips
  5. 05
    Opening shotsAnimate the first shots: the world, then the hero’s entrance.AI skill · keeping the hero the same from shot to shot
    Opening shots
  6. 06
    The action sceneAnimate the big moment in the middle of the story. Up to three takes per shot, then choose the best one.AI skill · directing: comparing takes and choosing one
    Middle shots
  7. 07
    The ending and the blooper huntAnimate the final shots, then hunt the glitches (faces that melt, extra legs, objects that appear) and fix or cut them. Short lesson: how to spot fake videos online.AI skill · AI video can be wrong or fake; how to spot a deepfake
    All shots done
  8. 08
    VoicesLearn voice acting (emotion, speed, pauses) and record their own lines on a headset. The narrator is their own voice or a preset AI voice.AI skill · synthetic voices, and why copying someone’s voice needs their consent
    Recorded voices
  9. 09
    Sound designMake sound effects from words (“door creak”, “whoosh”) and background sound like desert wind or a busy souq. Music only if the school allows it.AI skill · making sound from a description, and layering sounds
    Sound layer
  10. 10
    Edit the filmPut the clips, voices and sounds on the timeline: trim, order, add transitions, check the pacing.AI skill · editing for rhythm and timing: what to cut
    Rough cut
  11. 11
    Titles, credits and test screeningAdd a title card, subtitles in English and Arabic, and end credits with “Made with AI: what I did, what AI did”. Partner screening: two stars and a wish, then fix.AI skill · crediting AI honestly
    Final film

    Film PremiereMake a film poster, screen the films for parents, awards. Teaser: in Term 3, your hero becomes a toy.AI skill · presenting your work and how it was made
    Premiered film

How a club session runs

One hour a week, same rhythm every time. The teacher follows the script; Candide runs the AI.

AI video takes a few minutes to make, so studio time runs around a render queue: children queue a shot and keep working while it renders.

  • Rule 1 · You are the directorYou decide what happens on screen; AI makes the shots.
  • Rule 2 · Three takesUp to three takes per shot. Improve the prompt between takes, then pick the best.
  • Rule 3 · Never wait for a renderQueue the shot, then move on to your next task.
  • Saved every weekEach week ends with a clip, a voice or a sound on their board, ready for the next one.

What children learn

Eight AI skills, learned by directing a film of their own.

Week 4 starts with this: same AI, two motion prompts. Children see for themselves that a vague prompt gets a glitchy clip, and that better words fix it.

  • How AI video worksIt guesses the frames between pictures. Week 1.
  • Adapting a storyFrom comic to film: what changes on screen. Week 2.
  • Planning before generatingA shot list and an animatic. Week 3.
  • Writing motion promptsWho, action, camera, speed. Weeks 4 to 6.
  • DirectingComparing takes and choosing the best one. Week 6.
  • Spotting glitches and fakesMelting faces, extra legs, deepfakes. Week 7.
  • Using voices and sounds responsiblyYour own voice, and consent for anyone else’s. Weeks 8 and 9.
  • Crediting AI honestlyWhat I did, and what AI did. Weeks 10 and 11.

Safety

Video and voice, with guardrails built in.

Every Term 1 rule still applies: filtered pictures, no characters that belong to someone else, teacher control. Video and voice add these.

  • Only drawn characters moveNo photo of a real person is ever animated, so deepfakes are ruled out by design.
  • No voice cloningChildren record their own voice; the narrator is a preset voice from a vetted library.
  • Private recordingsParents consent before week 8, and recordings are deleted at the end of the school year.
  • Music off by defaultThe school chooses: no music, percussion and voice only, or an instrumental library.

Week 12 · Film Premiere

A cinema night for parents.

Parents watch their child’s film on a big screen. Candide supplies the class compilation video, the poster and certificate templates, award cards and the invitation.

  • The screeningEvery film in the class, back to back, in the auditorium or the classroom.
  • Film postersEach child makes a poster, printed and shown at the entrance.
  • Awards for every childBest Film, Best Voice Acting, Best Sound, Funniest Moment, and more.
  • Parent invitationIn English and Arabic, plus a private film page to share with family.
