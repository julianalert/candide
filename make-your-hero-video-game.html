Week by week

Five phases, one mission a week.

Design it, make it work, make it good, make it ready, Arcade Day. Every week ends with a playable version saved as a checkpoint.

  1. 01
    What is a game?Play three short games and find their parts: player, goal, obstacles, rules, score, win and lose. Unplugged: “program” a classmate robot with exact instructions. Fill in the game design card.Skill · computers follow exact rules, word for word
    Game design card
  2. 02
    Your first game in one messageDescribe the game in one message and watch it appear in the play box. Then change one thing at a time: speed, colours, size.Skill · building through conversation, one change per message
    Game, version 0
  3. 03
    Bring your hero inTurn the character sheet into a game sprite and the comic’s places into backgrounds, then put them in the game.Skill · pictures are separate from the rules that move them
    Game starring the hero
  4. 04
    Controls and rulesOn paper first, write the rules as “when… then…”: when I press space, the hero jumps. Then ask the AI for each rule and test it.Skill · events and conditions
    Hero moves, rules work
  5. 05
    Score, lives and timerAdd a score, lives or a timer, a start screen, and win and game-over screens.Skill · variables: things the game remembers and changes
    Start, play, end
  6. 06
    Enter the villainBring in the villain from Adventure 1 as the boss, plus obstacles. Describe exactly how it moves: “left and right, faster every 10 seconds”.Skill · describing behaviour precisely
    A real challenge
  7. 07
    Bug huntPlay to break the game, then report each bug like a detective: what I did, what happened, what should happen. Go back to a checkpoint when a fix breaks something else.Skill · debugging: AI’s code has mistakes too
    Bug-free version
  8. 08
    Levels and difficultyAdd level 2, and level 3 for ages 10–11. A partner plays: how many tries to win? Too easy or too hard? Adjust.Skill · game balance, and testing with numbers
    2–3 levels
  9. 09
    Sound and sparkleAdd sound effects from Adventure 2, sparkles when collecting, a shake when hit, and a title screen with the game’s name.Skill · small effects make a game feel good
    Polished game
  10. 10
    Playtest dayClassmates play each game and fill in a feedback card: how fun, too hard, bugs found. Then fix the top two problems.Skill · real players’ feedback beats your own opinion
    Version 1.0
  11. 11
    Peek at the codeOpen the code view, find the score or speed number and change it by hand. Make the cover, the instructions and the credits: “I designed…, AI wrote the code”.Skill · reading real code, and crediting AI honestly
    Final game
  12. 12
    Game Arcade DayEvery game on a screen, parents play, high-score boards, awards, and the year portfolio.Skill · presenting your game and how AI helped
    Published game

How a session runs

One hour a week, same rhythm every time. The teacher follows the script; Candide runs the AI.

Children describe a change, the AI updates the game, they press Play and test. The last ten minutes become a playtest.

Mini-lesson · 10 min

    01 · Warm-up · 5 min

    Kids join and focus

    A one-minute mini-game on the class screen. Children spot one game idea: a rule, a variable or a bug.

    02 · Mini-lesson · 10 min

    The teacher builds one change live

    The week’s short video, then the teacher makes one change in the chat on the class screen to show the mission.

  3. 03 · Studio time · 35 min

    Every child builds

    They describe one change, the AI updates the game, they press Play and test. Then the next change.

  4. Playtest Swap seats. Play. Give one tip. Save a checkpoint
    04 · Playtest · 10 min

    Swap seats and play

    Each child plays a neighbour’s game and gives one tip. Everyone saves a checkpoint before leaving.

  • Rule 1 · One change per messageSmall steps are easier for AI to get right, and for you to test.
  • Rule 2 · Play after every changeNever stack changes without testing them.
  • Rule 3 · Save when it worksMake a checkpoint before trying something big.
  • Rule 4 · Report bugs like a detectiveWhat you did, what happened, what should have happened.

What children learn

Eight skills, learned by building a game of their own.

Week 7 starts with this: the same bug, reported two ways. Children see for themselves that the AI can only fix what they describe clearly.

  • Giving exact instructionsComputers follow them word for word. Week 1.
  • Building through conversationOne change per message. Weeks 2 and 3.
  • Events and conditions“When I press space, the hero jumps.” Week 4.
  • VariablesScore, lives and a timer the game remembers. Week 5.
  • Describing behaviour preciselyExactly how the villain moves. Week 6.
  • DebuggingClear bug reports, and going back to a checkpoint. Week 7.
  • Testing with real playersToo easy or too hard? Count the tries. Weeks 8 and 10.
  • Reading code, crediting AIChange a number by hand; say what AI wrote. Week 11.

Safety

A game builder with the guardrails built in.

Every rule from Adventures 1 and 2 still applies. Games and AI-written code add these.

  • Single-player onlyNo multiplayer, no chat between players, no accounts inside games.
  • A sealed play boxGames run with no internet access: no links, no outside content, no data collection.
  • Cartoon conflict onlyEnemies get bonked and vanish in a sparkle. No blood, no weapons, no “kill”.
  • No gambling, no shopsNo loot boxes, spinning wheels, betting or fake shops.
  • No copies of famous gamesNames of well-known games and characters are blocked in the chat.
  • A builder that stays on topicIt only talks about building the game, and the teacher sees every chat.
  • Private sharingGame links are shared only after the teacher approves, never on public game sites.
  • Games with an endingShort games, with no endless loops built to keep children playing.

Week 12 · Game Arcade Day

Parents don’t just watch. They play.

The classroom becomes an arcade and every child’s game runs on its own station. Candide supplies the class arcade page, cover cards, stamp cards, awards, certificates, the portfolio cover and the invitation in English and Arabic.

  • Arcade stationsEach game on a laptop or tablet, with its printed cover card.
  • Parents playAn arcade card, at least five games to play, and a stamp at each one.
  • High-score boardsEach game shows the day’s top scores, and kids defend their record.
  • Awards for every childMost Fun Game, Hardest Boss, Best Hero Design, Bug Squasher, and more.
