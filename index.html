How a mission runs

One lesson. Four parts. Every child makes something.

The teacher runs the room. Candide runs the AI. Each mission follows the same rhythm, so after the first one the class knows exactly what to do.

  1. 01 · 5 min

    Hook

    The teacher projects the launch page: the class code, the join address and a short hook script to read aloud. Children join with the code and their first name.

  2. 02 · 25 min

    Build

    Each child works through four steps in their own workspace: a chat with an AI helper that plays a role, a picture button, and a board for the work they want to keep.

  3. 03 · 10 min

    Share

    The teacher opens the class gallery on the projector. Every pinned creation, grouped by first name, with a full-screen view for the one being discussed.

  4. 04 · 5 min

    Debrief

    One question from the brief, for example: when the picture was wrong, was the AI wrong or was the prompt unclear? That question is the point of the lesson.

Twenty-five of the forty-five minutes are hands-on with AI. The rest is the teacher’s: the hook, the share and the debrief, all scripted in the mission brief.

Works on Chromebooks, iPads and laptops · One live session per class · Ends automatically after three hours

Safety by design

Every message passes the same guardrails.

Children never talk to a raw chatbot. Each mission runs inside a layer of rules the child cannot change, with checks on the way in and on the way out.

  1. 01

    A fixed safety layer sits above every mission: no personal information, no real people as characters, no topics that don’t belong in a classroom. Nothing a child writes can move it.

  2. 02

    Every child message is checked before it reaches the AI, and every reply is checked before a child sees it. A blocked message gets one gentle line: “Let’s keep it about the mission.”

  3. 03

    Each child has a budget per mission, for messages and for pictures, and each school has a daily picture cap. The AI stays a tool in the lesson, not a game.

  4. 04

    Pictures are generated from a rewritten, safe version of the child’s words, with a style preset and no real people.

  • No child accountsA class code and a first name, kept per session. No email, no date of birth, no photo, no password.
  • Private creationsPictures live in a private store. Gallery links are unguessable and expire; the teacher can issue a new one.
  • Transcripts for safety review onlyKept 90 days, then deleted automatically. Pinned creations are kept until the school asks us to remove them.
  • Hosted in the EUNo analytics on children’s pages. Children’s data is never used to train AI models. A one-page data sheet comes with every pilot.

What children learn

Creating something exciting takes real thinking.

Every mission gives children a reason to practise skills they can use beyond the lesson.

Express an idea clearly.

Turn a picture in their head into words precise enough for someone else, or something else, to act on.

Think critically about AI.

Discover that AI doesn’t always get things right. Compare results, spot mistakes, check a fact, and decide what to keep.

Keep going when it doesn’t work.

Try another approach, fix the prompt, rewrite a line. Experience how an idea improves through practice.

Share their work with confidence.

Pin what they are proud of, see it on the projector, and explain the choices behind it.

Along the way, they learn the basics of using AI creatively—and how to recognise the difference between their own decisions and the tool’s contribution.

Your questions

Is Candide right for our school?

Do teachers need any AI experience?

No. Every mission comes with a brief: a hook to read aloud, the four build steps, how to run the share, the debrief question, and what to do if a class needs a stretch or a simpler version. The platform handles the AI.

What devices do the children need?

One per child, with a browser: a Chromebook, iPad or laptop. There is nothing to install. The workspace is designed for a 1024-pixel-wide screen, with large buttons and no hover-only actions.

Do children need accounts or email addresses?

No. They join with the class code and a first name, which is kept only for that session. No email, no password, no date of birth, no photo.

What can the AI see and say?

Only what the child types in that session, inside a fixed safety layer and the mission’s own instructions. Each message is checked before it reaches the AI and each reply is checked before the child sees it. The AI never asks for personal information, never writes about real people, and redirects anything off-mission.

Which AI models do you use?

Claude, by Anthropic, for the conversation and the safety checks, and a separate image model with a safety tier for pictures. Children’s data is not used to train any model.

Does a mission fit in one lesson?

Yes. Forty-five minutes: about five for the hook, twenty-five to build, ten to share and five to debrief. A session stays open for three hours in case a class runs over, then closes itself.

Where is the data hosted, and for how long?

In the EU. Transcripts are kept for 90 days for safety review, then deleted. Pinned creations and galleries stay until the school asks for them to be removed, which a school admin can do in one click.

How much does a pilot cost?

Pilots are priced per school and invoiced once. Tell us about your school and we’ll share the figure with the pilot plan.
