Term 1 · Create Your Comic

Twelve weeks. Every child publishes their own comic.

Each child invents an original hero and publishes a printed comic of 4 to 8 pages, learning eight AI skills along the way. The child is the author; AI is their illustrator and editor.

Safety by design

Children never touch an open AI model.

Every mission runs inside rules the child cannot change, with a check on the way in and another on the way out. Safety is built into the platform, not left to the teacher.

  1. The child writesOr speaks, with the microphone button.
  2. Checked on the way inBefore anything reaches the AI. Off-mission messages get one gentle line.
  3. The AI helper answersIn the week’s role, within a per-child budget.

    Fixed safety layer: no personal information, no real people, no copyrighted characters, nothing that doesn’t belong in a school club.

  4. Checked on the way outEvery reply, before the child sees it. Pictures come from a rewritten, safe prompt, modest by default.
  5. The child reads, and keeps goingThe AI stays a tool in their hands, not a game.
  • No child accountsA club code and their first name on the club list. No email, no password, no selfies.
  • Nothing leaves the clubNo chat between children, no public sharing. Gallery links are private.
  • Teacher in controlThe teacher sees every child’s work and can end the session at any time.
  • Hosted in the EUNever used to train AI models. Transcripts deleted after 90 days.

Your questions

Is the AI Creators Club right for our school?

Does the teacher who leads the club need any AI experience?

No. Every week’s mission comes with a script on the class screen: the warm-up, the short video and demo, how to explain the mission, what to look for during studio time, and how to run share and save. The platform handles the AI, and the teacher learns alongside the kids.

How does it fit our activities programme?

It’s a club like your others: one hour a week for a term, twelve sessions, during an activity slot or after school. For a ten-week term there is a shorter version that merges four of the weeks. It sits outside your official curriculum, so there’s nothing to change in the timetable.

Which ages is it for?

Ages 8 to 11, in two tracks. Ages 8–9 make a 4-page comic with a hero and a sidekick, using a guided prompt builder and read-aloud. Ages 10–11 make 6 to 8 pages with a villain and a plot twist, and write their prompts freely.

What happens at the end of term?

Comic Con. Each child presents their comic in one minute to the club and to parents, gets a printed A5 copy and a “Published Author & AI Creator” certificate, and the club’s comics become an anthology for your library. It’s the moment parents remember, and the reason the club fills again next term.

Do children need accounts or email addresses?

No. The teacher types the children’s first names once. Each week, children type the club code and tap their name, and their work from last week is there. No email, no password, no date of birth. They only ever upload photos of their paper sketches, never of themselves.

What can the AI see and say?

Only what the child types in the club, inside a fixed safety layer and the week’s own instructions. Each message is checked before it reaches the AI and each reply is checked before the child sees it. The AI never asks for personal information, never draws real people or characters that belong to someone else, and redirects anything off-mission.

Which AI models do you use?

Claude, by Anthropic, for the conversation and the safety checks, and a separate image model with a safety tier for pictures. Children’s data is not used to train any model.

Where is the data hosted, and for how long?

In the EU. Transcripts are kept for 90 days for safety review, then deleted. Children’s creations stay until the school asks for them to be removed, which a school admin can do in one click.

What comes after Term 1?

Children keep the same hero all year. In Term 2 their hero comes to life as an animation or a game; in Term 3 they design the toy and pitch it. Term 1 is available now; the next terms follow.
